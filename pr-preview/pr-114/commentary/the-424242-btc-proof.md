The section is an exchange speaking. Three days earlier an attacker had used a compromised administrator account to credit themselves coins in Mt. Gox's database and sell them into the order book, printing bitcoin at $0.01 and closing the exchange; the balances were rolled back, roughly 2,000 ₿ were reported actually withdrawn, and none of it — the fake coins, the crash, the rollback — touched the chain at all. With solvency in open doubt, Mark Karpelès offered to move a uniquely identifiable amount as proof the exchange still held its coins, and chose 424,242.42424242. A signature is the only sentence the chain can verify, so the answer to the doubt was this page.

The proof was real and it was bounded, and both halves are the lesson.
