The generic burn address, in circulation on Bitcointalk since 2011, its name its own warning label. An address is the hash of a key; this one's base58 text was chosen first — "BitcoinEaterAddressDontSend," padded to a valid checksum — so there is no key behind it and never was. Finding one would mean inverting the hash, and that difficulty is not a side effect but the security assumption every ordinary address rests on: the Eater is spendable exactly when everyone's coins are.

What the chain shows is one-way traffic: by this book's reading (2 August 2026), 13.41710835 ₿ across 5,793 arrivals and not one departure — protest burns, message burns, tests, and mistakes, indistinguishable on the page. A 2025 survey of burn addresses (arXiv:2503.14057) counts it among the three that hold over 99% of all provably burned coin. That any single arrival was deliberate is a claim its sender rarely signs; the ledger keeps only the arithmetic.
