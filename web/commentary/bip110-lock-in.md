Lock-in changes nothing a reader can see and everything a reader can expect. No transaction written after this chapter is read differently for it; no script becomes invalid; no output changes shape. What is settled here is the future — that the rule *will* bind at the activation height, and that the chapters between the two are the last written under the old terms.

The interval is the point. A soft fork with a lock-in gives everyone who writes transactions a book's worth of chapters to notice that what they are writing will soon mean something stricter, and to stop writing it. Read that way, lock-in is the loudest thing in this appendix: it is the chain saying, in advance, what it is about to refuse.
